arXiv 原文摘要

Low-resolution global navigation satellite system snapshot receivers face strong coupling among automatic gain control compression, coarse quantization, and waveform distortion from nonlinear interference mitigation. This paper presents Cross-Risk Interference-Resilient Snapshot Positioning, denoted CRISP, an aided single-snapshot architecture that allocates and fuses complementary pre-correlation processors over a bounded position-clock search region under a shared direct position estimation solve budget. On a pre-frozen baseband descriptor partition, offline calibration estimates uncentered second-order position-error cross moments and solves cardinality-constrained simplex programs along east, north, and up axes, retaining validation-screened multibranch combinations while assigning single matched processors elsewhere. An online reliability gate screens the same quantized snapshot to route unfamiliar waveforms toward single-branch protective operators. We characterize profiled contrast shifts after nonlinear suppression and derive a conditional finite-sample excess-risk bound across disjoint splits alongside an exact same-snapshot gating risk identity. Across 17 568 simulated 2-ms GPS L1 C/A trials, CRISP reduces core positioning root-mean-square error by 13.84%, > 100-m gross errors by 41.32%, and multi-geometry error by 12.23% over Snapshot-FD at 1.43 core and 1.51 overall solves per snapshot.
