arXiv 原文摘要

Radio emission generated on a planetary surface must cross the planet's ionosphere before escaping into space: below the local plasma frequency it is reflected back towards the surface. We investigate this process for broadband radio technosignatures (BRaTs) using a Chapman-layer description of photoionised atmospheres. The plasma frequency depends only weakly on stellar XUV irradiation, scaling as $Q_{\infty}^{1/4}$, implying turnover frequencies of 5-40 MHz for most terrestrial habitable-zone planets, potentially rising towards 100 MHz in extreme environments. The basic signature is simple: the continuum of a surface-origin BRaT should be strongly suppressed below a characteristic frequency that can be estimated from the XUV output of the host star, at every orbital phase and for any viewing geometry. Oblique propagation, magnetic fields, and collisional absorption broaden the cut-off into a smooth escape function, $f_{\mathrm{esc}}(ν,φ)$, where $φ$ is the orbital phase. Because the day-night ionisation pattern is anchored to the substellar point rather than to the rotating surface, disc integration adds a second, more discriminating signature: the half-escape frequency $ν_{1/2}$ varies predictably (by about 40-50 per cent over the orbit, depending on the angular emission pattern of the leakage) and remains locked to the orbital ephemeris. Finite atmospheric response times introduce modest phase lags but preserve the modulation. A low-frequency turnover alone is not unique to technology, but one that varies periodically with the orbital period would be difficult to reproduce through known astrophysical processes. Although observationally challenging, the ionospheric cut-off is a potential technosignature diagnostic and a novel probe of exoplanet ionospheres.
