arXiv 原文摘要

Accurate and robust estimation of battery state-of-health (SOH) and remaining useful life (RUL) is essential for safe and cost-effective operation of electrified transportation systems, yet it remains challenging under operating variability, non-stationary aging, and non-Gaussian measurement disturbances. This paper proposes Multi-scale Adaptive Robust State Estimation (MARS), a multi-scale adaptive robust state-estimation framework that couples a dual-branch temporal encoder with an equivalent-circuit-model-constrained differentiable generalized-correntropy adaptive Kalman filter. The encoder disentangles fast intra-cycle voltage-current-temperature dynamics from slow inter-cycle degradation, and the fused degradation context adaptively parameterizes the cycle-level process covariance, measurement covariance, and correntropy bandwidth. Through the unrolled robust filtering recursion, MARS performs physics-consistent state propagation and generalized-correntropy-based innovation correction, while uncertainty decomposition and Monte Carlo dropout provide calibrated prediction intervals for SOH estimation and conditional RUL prediction. Experiments on the NASA and CALCE benchmarks demonstrate improved SOH accuracy, achieving mean absolute error/root mean square error values of 0.72%/0.92% and 0.63%/0.80%, respectively, together with competitive conditional RUL performance against representative data-driven and hybrid baselines. The resulting conditional-RUL prediction intervals achieve coverage probabilities of 100.00% and 99.24% on NASA and CALCE, respectively. Ablation, disturbance-sensitivity, near-end-of-life, and runtime analyses further verify the contribution of each module and the practical inference cost for online battery management.
