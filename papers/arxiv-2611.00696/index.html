原文摘要 · Abstract

In urban environments, global navigation satellite system (GNSS) positioning is often degraded by signal blockage and reflection caused by surrounding buildings. To address this problem, various three-dimensional mapping-aided (3DMA) GNSS techniques have been proposed. Among them, zonotope shadow and reflection matching (ZSRM) has demonstrated effective positioning performance by determining the candidate receiver position set using predicted GNSS shadow and reflection regions. However, ZSRM remains vulnerable to satellite signal misclassification, which may lead to positioning failure. To address this limitation, this paper proposes a conservative candidate set determination strategy for robust ZSRM positioning. Instead of using only the most probable signal reception condition from an artificial intelligence (AI)-based classifier, the proposed method combines the candidate regions associated with the two most probable classes. This allows the candidate receiver position set to be determined more conservatively, reducing the risk of positioning failure under signal misclassification. The proposed method was evaluated using real-world urban GNSS data. Experimental results show that the proposed method improved the positioning success rate to 100% for RF, GBDT, and SVM classifiers, compared with 8.2%-30.1% for the existing ZSRM algorithm using individual classifier outputs and 96.6% for ZSRM with unanimous voting. Although the proposed method increased the position bounds by retaining additional candidate regions, it produced smaller position bounds than ZSRM with unanimous voting. These results demonstrate that the proposed method substantially enhances the robustness of ZSRM under satellite signal misclassification in urban GNSS environments.
